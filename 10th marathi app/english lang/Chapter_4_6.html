(a) Both were brilliant but poor. Both were dedicated to science. Marie topped her class with Honours in Physics and Mathematics despite poverty and hunger. Pierre was also a brilliant scientist who shared Marie's passion for research. (b) Both were equally great in their own ways. Pierre supported Marie's work and shared the Nobel Prize with her. However, Marie's courage to continue alone after Pierre's death and win a second Nobel Prize shows her exceptional strength. She is arguably greater because she overcame extreme poverty, gender discrimination and personal loss. (c) Pitchblende was extremely expensive and the Curies were very poor. The gift of one ton from the Emperor of Austria was the most precious gift they had ever received. It allowed them to continue their research without worrying about the cost of raw material. (d) Besides treating cancer, radium is used in radiotherapy for various tumours, in sterilizing medical equipment, in radiography to detect fractures, and in nuclear medicine for diagnostic imaging. (e) The Curies could have patented their discovery of radium and become extremely rich. But Marie refused to do so and gave it freely to the world, saying "Radium belongs to the people, not to me." She remained comparatively poor throughout her life.

Introduction: The maxim "There is no shortcut to success" means that lasting success can only be achieved through hard work, dedication and perseverance. There are no easy paths or magic formulas. Body: History proves this. Marie Curie worked tirelessly for years in a shabby shed, boiling pitchblende in extreme conditions before discovering radium. She faced poverty, hunger and health issues but never gave up. Similarly, great scientists, athletes and leaders achieved success through continuous effort. Mary Kom trained hard for years, balancing family and sport, before becoming a world champion. Students who top exams do so through regular study, not last-minute cramming. Shortcuts may give temporary gains but never lasting success. Those who try to cheat or take easy routes eventually fail. Real success requires patience, sacrifice and the willingness to work when others are resting. Conclusion: There is truly no elevator to success — you have to take the stairs, one step at a time. As Thomas Edison said, "There is no substitute for hard work."

Five Great Scientists and Their Inventions: 1. Marie Curie (1867–1934): Discovered radium and polonium; pioneered research on radioactivity; won two Nobel Prizes. 2. Albert Einstein (1879–1955): Developed the Theory of Relativity (E=mc²); made breakthrough contributions to quantum mechanics. 3. Isaac Newton (1643–1727): Discovered laws of motion and universal gravitation; invented calculus. 4. Thomas Edison (1847–1931): Invented the electric light bulb, phonograph, and motion picture camera; held over 1000 patents. 5. C.V. Raman (1888–1970): Discovered the Raman Effect (scattering of light); won Nobel Prize in Physics (1930); founded the Raman Research Institute. Each of these scientists worked with dedication, faced challenges, and contributed immensely to human progress.
