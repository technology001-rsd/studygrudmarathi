subjective
✅ Answer:
Title: A Teenager's Prayer Poet: J. Morse Rhyme scheme: abcb Favourite line: "Help me stand for what is right." The poem is a sincere prayer of a teenager who seeks God's guidance during the confusing years of adolescence. The central idea is that every new day brings new beginnings and decisions, and the speaker must choose between the "road of life" that leads to success and the "darkened road" that leads to distress. The poet prays to the Lord to open his eyes, to help him stand for what is right, to say "no" to temptation, and to keep his body clean and fit for life. The tone is humble, prayerful and hopeful. Figures of speech used include Apostrophe (addressing the Lord directly), Inversion ("Decisions, I must make"), Metaphor ("darkened road" for evil/wrong path) and Alliteration ("life is lived its very best"). The language is simple, musical and devotional, and the poem is written in quatrains with a regular rhythm that makes it easy to recite. I like this poem because it honestly expresses the fears and hopes of every teenager and shows that prayer, self-discipline and the right choices can lead to a meaningful life. It teaches us to walk with God and live our teenage years at their very best.
