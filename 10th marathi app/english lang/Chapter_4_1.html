4.1 The World is Mine

Joy Lovelet Crawford

(a) The poet saw a lovely girl hobble down the aisle (on the bus). (b) The poet wanted to buy sweets from the lad. (c) The poet told the boy to join others to play but he couldn't hear. (d) The poet requests God to forgive him.
objective
✅ Answer:
(a) The beautiful girl → (iv) had a handicap in her legs. (b) The lad who was selling sweets → (iii) was very thankful to the speaker. (c) The child, watching others play → (ii) was hearing impaired. (d) The poet says 'The World is Mine' → (i) he can experience the beauty and gifts of his life.
subjective
✅ Answer:
The poet means that she is immensely blessed despite not realizing it before. When she compares herself to people with disabilities — a girl with one leg, a blind lad, a deaf boy — she realizes how fortunate she is to have two legs, two eyes and two ears. The title "The World is Mine" signifies that true wealth is the ability to see, hear, walk and experience the beauty of life. We often complain about small things, forgetting that many people have far greater challenges. The title reminds us to be grateful for what we have.
subjective
✅ Answer:
(a) Theme: The poem teaches us to be grateful for our abilities and blessings instead of complaining. By comparing ourselves with those who are less fortunate, we realize how rich our lives truly are. The central message is gratitude and appreciation for God's gifts. (b) No, people with handicaps should not beg for money. They have talents and abilities that can be developed. With proper education, training and encouragement, they can lead dignified, independent lives. Society should provide them with equal opportunities, not charity. (c) It is better to encourage a disabled person to do things himself/herself in a different way. Taking over completely makes them dependent and lowers their self-esteem. Encouraging independence builds confidence and self-respect. We should support them, not do things for them.

activity
✅ Answer:
Student 1 (unable to walk): Do you know how many problems I have to face when I decide to go anywhere? I can't climb stairs easily and I need help to move around. Student 2 (can't hear): I can't hear anything. When people talk, I miss most of the conversation. I feel left out during group discussions. Student 3 (can't see): So sad! You both can't walk or hear, but my problem is more serious than yours. You know, I can't see this beautiful world. I can't see the sun, the trees, the faces of my loved ones. Student 1: I understand. But we must support each other. Student 2: (leaning forward) What did you say? Can you write it down? Student 3: Let's help each other. I'll speak for you, you can guide me while walking. Together we are strong!
activity
✅ Answer:
Title: Together We Stand A polio victim named Rohan and a strong but visually challenged man named Arun were close friends. They wished to go to a temple three kilometres away to pay homage. Rohan could not walk properly and Arun could not see. But they did not give up. Rohan sat on Arun's shoulders and guided him, "Turn left... now go straight... there's a stone ahead." In this way, they supported each other. Arun walked steadily with Rohan's directions, and Rohan rode on Arun's strong shoulders. They crossed the road, walked through the market, and reached the temple after an hour. They prayed together and felt immense joy. Their teamwork had made the impossible possible. Moral: "Unity is strength." / "Where there is a will, there is a way." Or: "In helping others, we help ourselves."
