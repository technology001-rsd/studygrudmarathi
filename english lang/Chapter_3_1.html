subjective
✅ Answer:
The poem begins each stanza with "If you can…" to present a series of conditions or challenges that lead to becoming a mature, virtuous person. This conditional structure (If… then…) creates a sense of progression and builds anticipation. The effect is that each stanza adds a new requirement, and the final stanza reveals the reward — "Yours is the Earth… you'll be a Man, my son!" This makes the reader reflect on their own character and aspire to meet these ideals.

objective
✅ Answer:
(a) The two impostors are Triumph (success) and Disaster (failure). The unforgiving minute can be made up of sixty seconds' worth of distance run (using every moment productively). (b) You should trust yourself when doubted. You can talk with crowds and walk with Kings (maintaining virtue with all types of people). (c) The Earth becomes yours when you fill every minute with worthwhile action and meet all conditions. You should start rebuilding with old tools when things you gave your life to are broken. (d) Knaves (dishonest people) twist the truth to make a trap for fools. You should consider all men equally important because every person counts with you, but none too much.
subjective
✅ Answer:
According to the poem, our attitude should be equal and balanced. We should be able to "talk with crowds and keep your virtue" (stay humble and good among common people) and also "walk with Kings, nor lose the common touch" (not become arrogant among the powerful). This means treating everyone with respect regardless of their social status, and maintaining our integrity in all company.
subjective
✅ Answer:
According to Kipling, the highest quality that makes a person a true human being is the ability to fill "the unforgiving minute with sixty seconds' worth of distance run" — meaning making the best use of every moment of life with discipline, patience, honesty, humility, courage and perseverance. When one meets all the conditions described in the poem, "Yours is the Earth and everything that's in it, and — which is more — you'll be a Man, my son!"
