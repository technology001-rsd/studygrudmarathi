✅ Answer:
(a) Name: The Boy Who Broke the Bank; Writer: Ruskin Bond. (b) Type: Humorous short story. (c) Setting: The fictional town of Pipalnagar, around the Pipalnagar Bank. (d) Main characters: Nathu (sweeper), Sitaram, Mrs. Srivastava, Mrs. Bhushan, Seth Govind Ram, the bank manager. (e) Problem: A small complaint grows into a rumour that the bank is collapsing, causing a panic and a riot. (f) Language: Simple, clear and funny English. (g) Liked: The gentle humour and how a tiny truth becomes a big rumour. (h) Disliked: The crowd turns violent at the end. (i) Opinion: A delightful story that teaches us not to believe every rumour.
subjective
✅ Answer:
Title: "The Kitchen Mystery" Evidence: There were children's footprints in the kitchen; the kitchen was messy with spilled flour; mother found empty jars of jam and biscuits. Possible explanation: The children came home hungry, entered the kitchen, made a mess while looking for snacks, and ate the jam and biscuits. Story: One afternoon, when mother was resting, her two children tiptoed into the kitchen. They found a jar of jam and a packet of biscuits and ate them happily, leaving footprints and a mess. When mother saw it, she laughed and asked them to help clean up. It was a small, sweet mischief.

✅ Answer:
Sample review of "The Blue Umbrella" by Ruskin Bond: Introduction: A short, heart-touching story set in a Himalayan village. Subject: A girl Binya and her blue umbrella. Sequence: Binya trades her leopard claw for an umbrella; a shopkeeper envies it; finally he gets it and she finds happiness. Language: Simple and descriptive. Good points: Beautiful nature description, gentle moral. Advantages: Teaches sharing and kindness. Demerits: Very short. Message: Possessions are less important than friendship. Conclusion: A lovely book for all ages.
