subjective
✅ Answer:
Summary: The poem is a funny account of identical twin brothers who looked so alike that even relatives could not tell them apart. At birth the nurse mixed them up, so the speaker was named John and John was named the speaker. At school the speaker was beaten for his brother's mistakes. Later, his intended bride married his brother by mistake. Even after death, he was buried as "brother John". The poem gently mocks how a "fatal likeness" creates endless confusion. Another title could be "The Mixed-Up Twins" or "A Case of Mistaken Identity".

✅ Answer:
Title: The Twins Poet: Henry Sambrooke Leigh Rhyme scheme: Couplets (aabb…), simple and regular. Favourite line: "My brother John got christened me, / And I got christened him." Theme: The humorous confusion caused by identical twins. Figures of speech: Alliteration ("kith and kin"), Pun ("taking me for him"), Hyperbole ("fearful pitch"). Special features: Light, comic tone; first-person narration; everyday language. I like this poem because it makes us laugh at a silly yet relatable situation and shows the fun of wordplay.
