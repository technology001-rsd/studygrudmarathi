subjective
✅ Answer:
(a) Nathu: a poor sweeper-boy at the Pipalnagar Bank; honest, hardworking, annoyed at not being paid, but still does his duty. (b) Mrs. Shrivastava: a wealthy lady who starts the rumour after hearing Nathu's complaint; talkative and fond of shopping. (c) Seth Govind Ram: the rich owner of the bank; away in Kashmir, unaware of the panic. (d) Sitaram: the washerman's son; friendly, helpful, carries clothes and spreads the news about Nathu to Mrs. Srivastava.
subjective
✅ Answer:
(a) Rumours are spread by fools and accepted by greater fools: A small complaint by a sweeper grew into a story that the bank had collapsed. People believed it without checking and rushed to take out money, breaking the bank. This shows how a careless word becomes a rumour, and how easily people believe what they hear. We must verify news before believing or spreading it. (b) Books are our real friends: Books never leave us; they give knowledge, joy and company. They improve our language, build confidence and keep us focused. Unlike false friends, books always guide us rightly. They are treasures of knowledge and true companions for life.
